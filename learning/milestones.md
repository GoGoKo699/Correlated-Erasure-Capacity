# Thirty milestones for correlated erasure capacity

The central question is how correlations among heralded erasures change the amount of quantum information transmitted per physical input qubit. This map follows that question from its physical objects and prerequisites to the complete supported scientific account. It covers the load-bearing ideas rather than every calculation or neighboring topic.

The single educational anchor is **Michael A. Nielsen and Isaac L. Chuang, *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information*, 10th Anniversary Edition, Cambridge University Press (2010)**. All references to **N&C** below name sections of this edition; their numbering is recorded in the [official contents][nc]. The [anchor and bridge](anchor-and-bridge.md) explains the notation and the steps supplied by the project. Primary papers are cited where the argument imports research-level results.

## Allocation and dependency structure

| Phase | Milestones | Count | Scientific role |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Background/preliminaries | M01–M12 | 12 | Join quantum subsystems and channels, entropy and capacity, and binary codes with incidence geometry into one communication model. |
| Narrative/innovation | M13–M30 | 18 | Develop the overlap converse, establish the extremal envelope, and follow the distinct geometric laws through exact recovery, local diagnostics, and additional loss. |

The allocation reflects the need to connect information-theoretic and code-based arguments. Twelve milestones establish their shared language; eighteen track how the arguments fit together without treating each calculation as a separate target. M12 integrates the foundations, and M30 integrates the complete scientific result. Dependencies list direct earlier prerequisites; their own prerequisites are inherited. The progression within each phase is from local objects and mechanisms to the global communication statement.

## Phase I: Background and preliminaries

### M01

**Subsystems and reference correlations**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What does it mean to transmit part of an entangled quantum system?

**Understanding target:** Interpret a carrier, a tensor-product input, a density operator, and the reduced state obtained by discarding carriers. Explain why a reference system is needed to express preservation of an unknown quantum state, including its correlations.

**Dependencies and story role:** None. Establishes the objects on which channels, entropy inequalities, and recovery statements act.

**Conceptual connection:** The same partial trace describes a receiver losing carriers and a reference observer checking whether quantum information survived.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§2.1.7, 2.4.3, 2.5][nc]; [Model §1](../science/model.md#1-one-channel-use-is-a-finite-block) and [§2](../science/model.md#2-operational-capacity-and-normalization).

### M02

**Channels and their environments**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** How can the receiver and the environment be described as two outputs of one physical process?

**Understanding target:** Explain a quantum channel through an isometric interaction followed by a partial trace. Identify the complementary channel as the output to the environment and distinguish it from an inverse or a recovery operation.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M01](#m01). Turns the subsystem picture into the open-system framework needed by the converse.

**Conceptual connection:** Coherent information will compare receiver and environment entropies generated by the same input and the same loss event.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§8.2.2–8.2.4, 8.3.1][nc]; [Model: complementary channel](../science/model.md#a-complementary-channel-that-keeps-the-stochastic-flag).

### M03

**Heralded erasure and the information in a flag**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What does the channel reveal when a specified collection of carriers is erased?

**Understanding target:** Read the subset-erasure map $`\mathcal E_\mu(\rho)=\bigoplus_S\mu(S)\rho_S`$. Distinguish the quantum state on retained coordinates from the classical mask specifying those coordinates. Explain input-independent mask selection and why the complementary channel also receives the flag.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M01](#m01), [M02](#m02). Fixes the local physical operation before comparing different laws of loss.

**Conceptual connection:** Heralding makes exact decoding conditional on a known retained set possible, while its input-independent randomness does not itself carry a logical message.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§8.2, 8.3.1][nc]; [Model §1, equations (1)–(3)](../science/model.md#1-one-channel-use-is-a-finite-block).

### M04

**Marginal loss, joint loss, and repeated blocks**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** Which features of a loss process are determined by individual survival probabilities?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish the one-site probabilities $`\alpha_i`$, pairwise independence, and the full law $`\mu`$ of a mask. Separate unrestricted correlations inside a block from independent repetitions of the whole block. Recognize that several different joint laws can share the same local statistics.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M03](#m03). Identifies the statistical freedom over which the capacity envelope is optimized.

**Conceptual connection:** The project changes correlations while holding physical marginal survival fixed; the repeated channel is still memoryless at the block level.

**Source pointers:** [N&C Appendix 1 and §8.1][nc]; [Model §1](../science/model.md#1-one-channel-use-is-a-finite-block); [Envelope §1](../science/capacity-envelope.md#1-quantifiers-and-the-universal-interval).

### M05

**Recovery accuracy and the charged communication resource**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What operational rate is being compared, and what counts as successful transmission?

**Understanding target:** Explain entanglement transmission with vanishing error, allowing an encoder to couple many blocks. Distinguish exact recovery in one block from an asymptotic capacity statement. Account for every physical input qubit and separate qubits per block from qubits per physical carrier.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M01](#m01), [M03](#m03), [M04](#m04). Provides the common resource and reliability standard for every subsequent bound.

**Conceptual connection:** A perfectly recovered logical qubit is a capacity lower bound only after its physical block size and repetition protocol have been specified.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§9.2.2, 9.3, 12.4][nc]; [Model §2](../science/model.md#2-operational-capacity-and-normalization).

### M06

**Entropy of quantum subsystems and classical flags**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** How is the information in a flagged quantum output divided between its classical and quantum parts?

**Understanding target:** Use the von Neumann entropy bound $`H(A_U)\le\sum_{i\in U}\log_2d_i`$ and the entropy formula for orthogonal flagged branches. Explain why a maximally mixed qubit contributes one bit of entropy and why the same flag entropy appears at both channel outputs.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M01](#m01), [M02](#m02), [M03](#m03). Connects the physical input budget to the entropic quantities used in the proof.

**Conceptual connection:** The dimension bound will turn an intersection of retained carriers into a numerical capacity upper bound.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§11.1, 11.3, 11.3.6][nc]; [Model §1, entropy formulas preceding equation (4)](../science/model.md#a-complementary-channel-that-keeps-the-stochastic-flag).

### M07

**Entropy inequalities and the role of purification**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** Which entropy constraints remain valid for arbitrary correlations among several quantum subsystems?

**Understanding target:** Explain subadditivity and strong subadditivity, and obtain weak monotonicity from strong subadditivity using a purification. Identify the usable form $`H(JL)+H(KL)\ge H(J)+H(K)`$ without assuming that $`J,K,L`$ are independent.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M01](#m01), [M06](#m06). Supplies the inequality that controls the parts of two masks outside their intersection.

**Conceptual connection:** Purification translates a familiar entropy theorem into the precise form needed when the environment holds erased subsystems.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§2.5, 11.3.4, 11.4][nc]; [Model §3, equations (7)–(8)](../science/model.md#3-a-two-set-entropy-inequality).

### M08

**Coherent information and regularized capacity**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** How does an entropy difference become an operational statement about quantum transmission?

**Understanding target:** Interpret coherent information as $`I_c=H(B)-H(E)`$ for a purified channel input. Explain the maximization over inputs and the regularization over arbitrarily many channel uses in the quantum-capacity theorem. Distinguish one-input coherent information, an achievable rate, and a capacity converse.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M02](#m02), [M05](#m05), [M06](#m06), [M07](#m07). States the research-level theorem needed to pass from entropy estimates to all allowed asymptotic codes.

**Conceptual connection:** The textbook supplies entropy exchange and data processing; the modern regularized capacity characterization is a separately cited primary result.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§12.4.1–12.4.2][nc]; [Devetak, D05][d05], quantum-capacity theorem; [Model §2, equation (5)](../science/model.md#2-operational-capacity-and-normalization).

### M09

**Exact erasure correction and complementary privacy**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What does perfect recovery from one subset imply about information in its complement?

**Understanding target:** Explain why recovery must preserve a reference and why, for a pure encoding, a complementary subsystem then has a fixed state independent of the logical input. Distinguish an authorized recovery set, a private set, and a set on which a particular decoder merely fails.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M01](#m01), [M02](#m02), [M05](#m05). Prepares the relation between geometric authorized sets and the later logical erasure channel.

**Conceptual connection:** Correctability and privacy are two consequences of the same factorization of the encoded logical system from its environment.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§2.5, 10.3][nc]; [Cleve–Gottesman–Lo, CGL99][cgl99]; [Geometry: privacy of proper subsets](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#lower-bound-and-privacy-of-proper-subsets).

### M10

**Binary linear codes, CSS codes, and logical operators**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** How can binary linear spaces specify a quantum code and its recoverable degrees of freedom?

**Understanding target:** Connect binary dot products with commutation of Pauli strings, nested linear spaces with CSS stabilizers, and independent stabilizer generators with the number of logical qubits. Explain that different physical representatives can implement the same logical operator and that Clifford gates transform these representatives.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M01](#m01), [M05](#m05), [M09](#m09). Builds the algebraic language shared by the imported storage codes and the explicit plane code.

**Conceptual connection:** The dimension of a protected subspace and the support of its logical algebra determine what a retained set can recover.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§10.4.1–10.4.2, 10.5.1–10.5.2, 10.5.8][nc]; [Geometry: complete stabilizer](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#pure-encoder-and-complete-stabilizer).

### M11

**Incidence geometry as a family of recovery sets**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** How do finite-field points and lines organize which physical qubits are retained together?

**Understanding target:** Interpret points of $`\mathrm{PG}(2,q)`$ as one-dimensional subspaces of $`\mathbb F_q^3`$, and lines as kernels of nonzero linear functionals. Explain the counts $`n=q^2+q+1`$, line size $`q+1`$, and one-point intersection of distinct lines. Separate the field used to label the geometry from the Hilbert-space dimension of a physical carrier.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M04](#m04), [M10](#m10). Adds the combinatorial setting needed to turn logical supports into explicit correlated-loss channels.

**Conceptual connection:** Incidence specifies both the authorized sets used by a code and the overlaps charged by the converse; the same geometry serves two roles.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §10.4.1][nc] for linear-code foundations; [Audoux–Couvreur, AC19 §4.1][ac19]; [Geometry: incidence geometry](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#incidence-geometry).

### M12

**Background synthesis: one common communication problem**

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What must be fixed before two correlated-loss constructions can be compared fairly?

**Understanding target:** Assemble the input dimensions, mask law, receiver information, independent block repetitions, unassisted resources, accuracy criterion, and rate normalization into one operational description. Explain the complementary roles of a concrete recovery code and an upper bound covering every allowed encoder.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M03](#m03), [M04](#m04), [M05](#m05), [M08](#m08), [M09](#m09), [M10](#m10), [M11](#m11). Closes the background phase by joining physical, statistical, entropic, and coding viewpoints.

**Conceptual connection:** The scientific narrative will use one converse to compare several fully specified channels, with each lower bound attached to its actual code and resource budget.

**Source pointers:** [N&C Chapters 8–12][nc]; [Model §§1–2](../science/model.md#1-one-channel-use-is-a-finite-block); [Envelope §1](../science/capacity-envelope.md#1-quantifiers-and-the-universal-interval); [Anchor bridge §3](anchor-and-bridge.md#3-notation-and-conventions).

## Phase II: Scientific narrative and innovation

### M13

**Coherent information averaged over masks**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why is coherent information an average of retained-versus-erased subsystem entropies?

**Understanding target:** Derive $`I_c(\rho,\mathcal E_\mu)=\sum_S\mu(S)[H(\rho_S)-H(\rho_{S^c})]`$ from the two flagged outputs. Track the cancellation of $`H(\mu)`$ and preserve the distinction between a distribution over masks and an assumption about the input state.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M03](#m03), [M06](#m06), [M08](#m08), [M12](#m12). Begins the argument with its exact single-block information quantity.

**Conceptual connection:** The operational channel becomes a probability-weighted set function to which a two-mask entropy comparison can be applied.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§11.3.6, 12.4.1][nc]; [Model §1, equation (4)](../science/model.md#a-complementary-channel-that-keeps-the-stochastic-flag).

### M14

**The two-mask entropy mechanism**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why can two retained-versus-erased entropy differences be bounded by their common retained subsystem?

**Understanding target:** Partition the carriers into $`S\cap T`$, $`S\setminus T`$, $`T\setminus S`$, and $`(S\cup T)^c`$. Combine subadditivity with weak monotonicity to obtain the bound by $`2H(\rho_{S\cap T})`$, explaining why it holds for every input and requires no exact decoder for either set.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M07](#m07), [M13](#m13). Isolates the entropy mechanism that makes a stochastic converse possible.

**Conceptual connection:** Geometric intersection appears because the contributions outside the shared subsystem cancel under a quantum entropy constraint.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§11.3.4, 11.4][nc]; [Model §3, equations (6)–(8)](../science/model.md#3-a-two-set-entropy-inequality).

### M15

**Self-couplings and the optimal overlap cost**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How can two mask draws be paired without changing either channel marginal?

**Understanding target:** Define a self-coupling $`\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\mu)`$ and the weighted cost $`C_\cap(\mu;w)=\min_\pi\mathbb E_\pi\sum_{i\in S\cap T}\log_2d_i`$. Explain the marginal constraints, the entropy-to-dimension step, and why minimizing a finite transport problem sharpens the upper bound.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M04](#m04), [M06](#m06), [M14](#m14). Turns the two-set inequality into a probability-sensitive optimization.

**Conceptual connection:** A coupling is a comparison of classical mask labels, not an operation that duplicates a quantum input; its freedom can exploit the structure of the actual loss law.

**Source pointers:** [N&C Appendix 1, §11.3][nc] for probability and entropy foundations; [Model §4, equations (9)–(11)](../science/model.md#4-optimal-overlap-transport).

### M16

**A converse for arbitrary many-block encoders**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why can entangling inputs across more channel uses not defeat the overlap bound?

**Understanding target:** Apply a product self-coupling to an arbitrary many-block input and use the intersection dimension bound to obtain $`I_c\le kC_\cap`$. Explain how regularization gives $`Q\le C_\cap`$. Prove that the transport cost itself is additive by projecting any joint coupling onto its block pairs, while keeping that statement separate from additivity of quantum capacity.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M08](#m08), [M15](#m15). Completes the universal operational converse before any channel-specific code is considered.

**Conceptual connection:** The proof factors the comparison distribution, not the quantum input; this is the step that preserves coverage of general encoders.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §12.4.2][nc] for data processing; [Devetak, D05][d05]; [Model §4, equation (12) and transport additivity](../science/model.md#4-optimal-overlap-transport).

### M17

**Sharp universal bounds from common marginals**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What can a common survival probability alone guarantee or rule out?

**Understanding target:** Obtain $`Q/n\le\alpha^2`$ from independent draws in the comparison coupling and $`Q/n\ge(2\alpha-1)_+`$ from the maximally mixed input. Explain why the first use of independence concerns the coupling, whereas independent physical erasures attain the lower extreme. Keep the common-marginal assumption distinct from knowing only an average survival probability.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M06](#m06), [M13](#m13), [M16](#m16). Identifies both universal edges of the interval that the constructions must address.

**Conceptual connection:** The same one-site statistics determine a lower guarantee and an upper ceiling, leaving a gap controlled by higher-order loss correlations.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§11.3, 12.4][nc]; [Bennett–DiVincenzo–Smolin, BDS97][bds97]; [Model §5](../science/model.md#5-consequences-from-marginal-survival-probabilities); [Envelope §1](../science/capacity-envelope.md#1-quantifiers-and-the-universal-interval).

### M18

**The inherited intersection-star storage code**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Which exact storage construction can approach the quadratic upper ceiling?

**Understanding target:** Describe nodes labelled by $`m`$-subsets of $`[\Delta]`$ and stars $`D_a=\{J:a\in J\}`$. State the imported Sun–Jafar recovery theorem with $`N=\binom\Delta m`$ physical field systems and $`K=\binom{\Delta-2}{m-2}`$ logical systems. Identify the sufficiently large binary extension field as a genuine construction dependency.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M10](#m10), [M11](#m11), [M12](#m12), [M17](#m17). Introduces the achieving family and its precise imported assumptions.

**Conceptual connection:** An exact storage code supplies a common encoder for every authorized star; the stochastic channel analysis will determine the ordinary capacity of random star selection.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§10.4.2, 12.4.3][nc] for code and intersection-bound foundations; [Sun–Jafar, SJ25 Theorem 6, §5.7 and Lemma 3][sj25]; [Envelope §2](../science/capacity-envelope.md#2-the-exact-storage-code-input).

### M19

**Exact ordinary capacity of uniform random stars**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why does the exact storage rate also equal the ordinary stochastic-channel capacity for this law?

**Understanding target:** Combine the inherited perfect decoder with a self-coupling of distinct uniform stars. Use $`|D_a\cap D_b|=K`$ to match the converse to $`K\log_2q`$ logical qubits per block and interpret the normalized rate $`m(m-1)/[\Delta(\Delta-1)]`$.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M15](#m15), [M16](#m16), [M18](#m18). Provides an exact family of capacities before taking an extremal limit.

**Conceptual connection:** The code gives achievability for every mask; the stochastic converse separately excludes improvement by general coding across random masks.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §12.4][nc]; [SJ25 Theorem 6][sj25] for the encoder; [Envelope §3, equations (10)–(13)](../science/capacity-envelope.md#3-exact-capacity-of-a-random-star-channel).

### M20

**Physical qubits and exactly prescribed survival**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How does a field-symbol construction become a valid qubit channel at any desired marginal survival?

**Understanding target:** Convert each $`q=2^r`$ dimensional node into exactly $`r`$ physical qubits, counting all $`Nr`$ inputs. For $`\alpha_0=m/\Delta\le\alpha`$ with $`\alpha_0<1`$, retain each previously erased whole node independently with probability $`(\alpha-\alpha_0)/(1-\alpha_0)`$. Explain why the final mask alone contains enough information to locate a complete authorized star and recover without the latent original index.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M03](#m03), [M05](#m05), [M18](#m18), [M19](#m19). Closes the dimension and exact-marginal gaps in the achieving construction.

**Conceptual connection:** Resource accounting and a decoder that only uses observed outputs make the abstract storage theorem a channel in the same optimization as the universal bound.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§2.1.7, 10.3][nc]; [Envelope §4](../science/capacity-envelope.md#4-conversion-to-an-actual-qubit-erasure-channel) and [§5](../science/capacity-envelope.md#5-exact-prescribed-marginals-by-extra-retention); [SJ25 §5.7][sj25].

### M21

**The complete extremal capacity envelope**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What does the equality of the supremal rate with $`\alpha^2`$ mean for finite physical channels?

**Understanding target:** Combine the universal bound with $`m=\lfloor\alpha\Delta\rfloor`$ and growing finite constructions to obtain $`F_+(\alpha)=\alpha^2`$ for every $`\alpha\in[0,1]`$. State the quantifiers: for every accuracy in rate there is a finite block with an exact code approaching the ceiling. Integrate this with $`F_-(\alpha)=(2\alpha-1)_+`$ without asserting finite-size attainment or an efficient construction.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M17](#m17), [M19](#m19), [M20](#m20). Synthesizes the first main branch: the sharp range of possible capacities at fixed marginals.

**Conceptual connection:** Matching upper and lower arguments answer an extremal question while preserving the distinction between a particular channel and a supremum over channels.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §12.4][nc] for the communication framework; [Envelope §§6–7](../science/capacity-envelope.md#6-the-supremal-envelope), especially equations (19)–(21); [Related work §2](../references/related-work.md#2-distributed-storage-the-closest-coding-antecedent).

### M22

**A binary plane code with exactly one logical qubit**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How do projective lines define the specific one-qubit code used by the geometric channels?

**Understanding target:** For $`q=2^m`$ with $`m\ge1`$, construct the line-difference space $`D`$ and $`C=D\oplus\langle v_0\rangle`$. Interpret the two coset-superposition codewords and the complete stabilizer $`X(D),Z(C^\perp)`$. Explain the count of $`n-1`$ independent stabilizers and why using $`X(D),Z(D)`$ alone would leave extra logical degrees of freedom for $`q>2`$.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M09](#m09), [M10](#m10), [M11](#m11), [M12](#m12). Starts the second construction branch with its full physical encoding specified.

**Conceptual connection:** An incidence identity over binary parity fixes both the protected logical dimension and the code whose recovery properties are later used.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§10.4.2, 10.5.1, 10.5.5][nc]; [AC19 §4.1, Definition 4.9][ac19]; [Geometry: pure encoder and complete stabilizer](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#pure-encoder-and-complete-stabilizer).

### M23

**Logical support and a decoder on one retained line**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why can any complete projective line recover the entire logical qubit?

**Understanding target:** Show that every line supports representatives of both logical Pauli operators. Explain the two sequences of CNOTs, using $`2q`$ gates within the retained line, and the role of its odd size $`q+1`$. Connect the transformed logical algebra to factorization of the recovered pivot qubit from an input-independent remainder, including a reference.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M09](#m09), [M10](#m10), [M22](#m22). Makes exact recovery constructive rather than relying only on an access-set label.

**Conceptual connection:** A geometric support statement becomes a local decoding operation with no access to the erased carriers.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§10.5.2, 10.5.8][nc]; [AC19 Lemma 4.11][ac19]; [Geometry: logical operators](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#logical-pauli-operators-on-every-line) and [explicit decoder](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#explicit-decoder-using-the-retained-line-alone).

### M24

**One qubit per uniformly selected line**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why is the uniform-line channel completely characterized by one logical qubit per block?

**Understanding target:** Match the exact line decoder with a coupling of distinct uniform lines whose intersection contains one qubit. Obtain $`Q=1`$, physical block size $`n=q^2+q+1`$, marginal survival $`(q+1)/n`$, and normalized rate $`1/n`$. Distinguish this ordinary-channel equality from the inherited exact-storage statement.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M16](#m16), [M22](#m22), [M23](#m23). Applies the common converse to the smaller binary geometric construction.

**Conceptual connection:** The same one-point incidence that supports nontrivial recovery also fixes the maximum ordinary communication rate.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§10.3, 12.4.3][nc]; [Geometry §2](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#2-uniform-line-channel-exact-capacity-one); [AC19 §4.1][ac19] and [SJ25 Theorem 5][sj25] for antecedents.

### M25

**Normal vectors and equality of local channel maps**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How can correlated erasures have exactly the same one- and two-carrier channels as independent loss?

**Understanding target:** Analyze a uniform $`a\in\mathbb F_q^3`$ and retain points satisfying $`a\cdot x=0`$, including the full-retention event $`a=0`$. Obtain survival $`1/q`$ and pair survival $`1/q^2`$. Explain equality of the selected local channel maps, including reference inputs, only after every unselected quantum output and flag is discarded.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M01](#m01), [M03](#m03), [M04](#m04), [M11](#m11), [M23](#m23), [M24](#m24). Defines a distinct mask law that isolates what local diagnostics can miss.

**Conceptual connection:** Linear independence of projective point representatives produces independent local flags while their common normal vector imposes higher-order structure.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§8.2, 8.4.2][nc] for channel maps and process tomography; [Geometry §3](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#3-uniform-normal-vectors-pairwise-independent-loss), especially [local flags and reduced channels](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#local-flags-and-reduced-channels).

### M26

**Near-optimal transmission behind identical local diagnostics**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How much capacity can remain when all one- and two-carrier loss diagnostics agree with a zero-capacity independent channel?

**Understanding target:** Explain $`1\le Q\le1+2/q^2`$ for the normal-vector channel, with the upper number an exactly solved transport cost rather than an established exact capacity. Relate the achievable rate $`1/(q^2+q+1)`$ to the $`\alpha^2`$ ceiling along $`\alpha=1/q`$, and identify collinear triple statistics or the full mask as witnesses of the global correlation.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M15](#m15), [M17](#m17), [M21](#m21), [M23](#m23), [M25](#m25). Connects the local-channel example to the extremal law and its precise remaining uncertainty.

**Conceptual connection:** Equal low-order channel maps permit sharply different communication behavior because the decoder accesses the whole retained pattern.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§8.4.2, 12.4][nc]; [BDS97][bds97]; [Geometry: capacity and sharp overlap bound](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#capacity-and-the-sharp-overlap-bound); [Envelope §7](../science/capacity-envelope.md#7-what-the-extremal-statement-establishes).

### M27

**Additional independent loss and the logical erasure channel**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What effective channel does the plane code produce when the selected line is thinned?

**Understanding target:** Specify uniform line selection followed by independent retention $`t`$ on that line, with only the final mask observed. Explain the full-line probability $`r=t^{q+1}`$, privacy of every proper retained subset, and the decoder producing an ordinary logical erasure channel. Obtain the lower rate $`[2r-1]_+`$ with all failed blocks counted.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M03](#m03), [M08](#m08), [M09](#m09), [M23](#m23), [M24](#m24). Introduces a controlled perturbation of the uniform-line law and its achievable capacity curve.

**Conceptual connection:** Exact recovery and complementary privacy reduce the chosen code to a familiar channel, while the ultimate capacity still requires a converse for other codes.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §§10.3, 12.4][nc]; [BDS97][bds97]; [Geometry §4](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#4-uniform-line-channel-followed-by-independent-thinning), especially [lower bound and privacy](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#lower-bound-and-privacy-of-proper-subsets).

### M28

**Normalized couplings for the observed proper masks**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How can the thinning law be coupled disjointly without revealing its unobserved originating line?

**Understanding target:** Express the proper-mask law as a mixture of the uniform-size laws $`\mu_k`$. Understand the disjoint proper/proper and full-line/proper couplings, including why their marginals remain correct. Track the aggregation of empty and singleton masks with multiple latent line representations.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M04](#m04), [M11](#m11), [M15](#m15), [M27](#m27). Supplies the probability-preserving constructions needed for a sharp thinning converse.

**Conceptual connection:** A convenient latent description may generate a coupling, but its pushforward must be a valid law on the receiver's actual observed masks.

**Source pointers:** [N&C Appendix 1][nc]; [Geometry: normalized component laws](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#normalized-component-laws), [proper-size self-couplings](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#disjoint-self-coupling-of-each-proper-size-law), and [full/proper coupling](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#disjoint-coupling-between-full-lines-and-proper-subsets).

### M29

**Exact thinning capacity and its loss threshold**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why is the code-induced lower bound optimal even among arbitrary encoders?

**Understanding target:** Assemble the component couplings separately for $`r\le1/2`$ and $`r>1/2`$, giving overlap costs zero and $`2r-1`$. Match the converse to $`Q=[2t^{q+1}-1]_+`$ and, writing $`\eta=1-t`$, interpret the additional-loss threshold $`\eta_c=1-2^{-1/(q+1)}`$. Keep this exact curve attached to the thinned uniform-line channel, distinct from the normal-vector law.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M16](#m16), [M26](#m26), [M27](#m27), [M28](#m28). Completes the second exact capacity family and quantifies the fragility of line recovery.

**Conceptual connection:** The one-shot success probability becomes an optimal asymptotic rate only after the same probability weights produce a matching all-encoder upper bound.

**Source pointers:** [N&C §12.4][nc]; [Geometry: assembled upper bound](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#assemble-the-capacity-upper-bound) and [additional-loss threshold](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#additional-loss-threshold).

### M30

**Scientific synthesis: correlations, capacity, and scope**

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What complete account of correlated erasure is supported by the converse and its matching constructions?

**Understanding target:** Connect the sharp marginal-capacity extremes, the exact star and line capacities, the local-diagnostic separation, and the exact thinning curve in one resource-consistent account. Identify the imported code and coding-theorem dependencies, the distinction between transport cost and capacity, and what analytical proofs establish beyond finite verification. Preserve the limits concerning independent block repetitions, finite-size attainment, and the unresolved exact normal-vector capacity.

**Dependencies and story role:** [M21](#m21), [M24](#m24), [M26](#m26), [M29](#m29). Closes the narrative phase by integrating the results, their mechanisms, and their limits.

**Conceptual connection:** Joint loss structure determines which quantum information can be recovered; entropy and overlap quantify the universal restrictions, and explicit codes show when those restrictions are sharp.

**Source pointers:** [N&C Chapters 8–12][nc]; [Science overview](../science/README.md); [Envelope §7](../science/capacity-envelope.md#7-what-the-extremal-statement-establishes); [Geometry §§2–5](../science/geometry-and-loss.md#2-uniform-line-channel-exact-capacity-one); [Verification scope](../verification/README.md#relation-to-the-proofs); [Attribution and source limits](../references/related-work.md); [Anchor bridge §4](anchor-and-bridge.md#4-the-additional-steps-supplied-by-this-project).
